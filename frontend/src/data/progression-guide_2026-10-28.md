# Double progression (with RIR)

Double progression: keep the weight the same and build reps toward the top of the rep range. Once you get there, add weight and build the reps back up. RIR (reps in reserve) is separate: it describes how hard each set is (~RIR 2 on compounds, ~RIR 1 on isolations in this program).

---

## The rules

1. Pick the right weight. Use the heaviest weight that keeps you inside the rep range with clean reps at the target RIR. Not the heaviest weight you can move — swinging or cutting reps short moves the work off the target muscle.
2. When to add weight: when your first 2 sets hit the top of the rep range at the target RIR. Later sets do not need to — fatigue builds across sets, and waiting for every set to hit the top can keep you stuck at one weight for months.
3. After adding weight, stay there. Reps will drop at the new weight — that is expected. Keep the new weight as long as your first 2 sets hit the bottom of the range, and build reps back up over the next few sessions.
4. When to drop back: only if your first 2 sets fall below the bottom of the range two sessions in a row, if form breaks down, or if a joint hurts. One bad day is not a reason to drop back.
5. Later sets can go lighter. If a late set would fall below the range, lowering the weight for that set is fine. It does not change the weight you start with next session.
6. Rep PRs count. Same weight, more total reps across your sets is progress.

## Example: Incline DB Press

From the program: 4 sets · 8–12 reps · RIR 2 (Upper B). Bottom of range is 8, top is 12.

| Session | DBs | Set 1 | Set 2 | Set 3 | Set 4 | Decision |
|--------|-----|-------|-------|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | 75 | 12 | 11 | 10 | 9 | Set 2 not at 12 — stay |
| 2 | 75 | 12 | 12 | 11 | 9 | First 2 sets at 12 → add weight |
| 3 | 80 | 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | Reps dropped, first 2 sets still ≥ 8 — stay |
| 4 | 80 | 10 | 10 | 9 | 8 | Stay |
| 5 | 80 | 11 | 11 | 9 | 8 | Stay |
| 6 | 80 | 12 | 12 | 10 | 9 | First 2 sets at 12 → add weight |
| 7 | 85 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 6 | Set 2 below 8 — stay, but watch it |
| 8 | 85 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 6 | Below 8 two sessions in a row → drop back to 80 |

Sessions won't always move forward; repeating a week is fine. In session 3, set 4 could have been done with 75s instead to stay in range.

## Big jumps on small lifts

Each ~3% increase in load costs about 1 rep. On small lifts, one dumbbell or pin step can be a 15–25% jump, which can knock you out of a 4-rep range entirely. That is why some lifts use a wider range:

| Exercise | Typical jump | Rep drop | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lateral raise | 16 → 20 lb (+25%) | ~7–8 reps | 12–20 |
| Hammer curl | 30 → 35 lb (+17%) | ~5–6 reps | 10–15 |
| EZ bar curl | 60 → 65 lb (+8%) | ~2–3 reps | 10–12 |

### Example: Cable Lateral Raise

From the program: 4 sets · 12–20 reps · RIR 1. Bottom of range is 12, top is 20.

| Session | Weight | Set 1 | Set 2 | Set 3 | Set 4 | Decision |
|--------|--------|-------|-------|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | 16 | 18 | 17 | 15 | 14 | Stay |
| 2 | 16 | 20 | 18 | 16 | 14 | Set 2 not at 20 — stay |
| 3 | 16 | 20 | 20 | 17 | 15 | First 2 sets at 20 → add weight |
| 4 | 20 | 13 | 12 | 11 | 10 | Big drop, but first 2 sets still ≥ 12 — stay |
| 5 | 20 | 14 | 13 | 12 | 11 | Stay |
| 6 | 20 | 16 | 15 | 13 | 12 | Stay |
| … | 20 | … | … | … | … | Keep building |
| 10 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 16 | 14 | First 2 sets at 20 → add weight |

Sets 3 and 4 never need to reach 20. If session 4 had come in at 11, 10 and then 11, 10 again, that is below 12 two sessions in a row — drop back to 16 and build again.

If you still keep bouncing between two weights, magnetic add-on plates (1.25–2.5 lb) make the steps smaller.

---

## RIR sanity check

Double progression says when to add weight. RIR says whether the weight is right:

- Logging RIR 3+ on sets that should be RIR 1–2? The weight is too light. Add weight even if you haven't hit the top of the range.
- Hitting RIR 0 at the bottom of the range right after a jump? The jump is too big. Use a smaller step if one exists, or go back and build more reps at the old weight first.

## Why "double"

Two steps that repeat: reps go up at the same weight, then weight goes up and reps reset. It doesn't mean progressing twice as fast.
